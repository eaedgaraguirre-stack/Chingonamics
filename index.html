The floor file

The Index is the billboard. The asset is who is actually on the lot — city, not “L.A.”; a floor fact; a public link. Letterhead does not count. Costume does not count.

Work a plant, port, aero lot, or shop? Send four lines to @chingonamics or lots@chingonamics.com.

  1. Firm
  2. City (Hawthorne, San Pedro, Palmdale — not “LA”)
  3. What’s on the lot
  4. Public source (permit, award, job listing, gate photo)

Southern California did not become worth crossing a border for because a studio had a good quarter. It became that because there were docks, assembly lines, tool rooms, ports, and steel in the ground.

Hollywood is real. It is also a costume. Underneath it is one of the country’s largest manufacturing workforces, the busiest container complex in North America, and a South Bay aerospace floor that still matters.

Chingonamics is proud of how chingón this place is — specifically for the children of immigrants who were told the only L.A. that counted was the one on a screen. The Index counts the lot.

Method, short version

Presence-weighted. Not headquarters-weighted. SpaceX can live in Texas on paper and still be a Hawthorne company in this book. Tips without a public source stay out of the file.

Composite, base year 2024 = 100:

  • 30% Jobs that pay — aero/defense, manufacturing, trade/transport, construction
  • 25% Metal and dirt — industrial absorption, plant permits, announced capex
  • 25% Contracts and capital — federal awards by place of performance, port TEUs, public hard-tech rounds
  • 20% The ladder — establishment births and apprenticeships when a public series exists

Gauges: Aerospace & Space · Plants · Ports & Movement · Building the Basin · Quiet Giants. Entertainment sits on a satellite strip called The Costume. It is never the core.

Above 100 = stronger than 2024. Below 90 = the costume is doing more work than the body. First live score ships as Issue 00 / ESTIMATE.
