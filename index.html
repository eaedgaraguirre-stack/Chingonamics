Thesis

Northern Mexico in 1846 was not empty. It was a military grid collapsing in place. After independence in 1821, an arc of presidios from Alta California to Texas remained on the map, but payroll, munitions, remounts, and roads thinned as Apache and Comanche pressure intensified.

Caminos mattered as much as forts: El Camino Real de Tierra Adentro and the Sonora–Tucson line were logistical lifelines that determined whether presidios could function as more than symbols.

By 1841 the minister of war described what remained as "immense deserts." The map survived. The supply did not. — Almonte, Memoria del Ministro de Guerra, January 1841.
